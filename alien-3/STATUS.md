# Alien 3 (USA) — what is known, and the experiment behind each claim

ROM: `~/roms/dendy/nes/Alien 3 (USA).nes` (read in place, never copied). iNES: mapper 4 (MMC3),
8 × 16 KB PRG, 16 × 8 KB CHR. PRG offsets below are past the 16-byte header; "B<n>:$addr" is PRG
8 KB bank n seen at CPU $addr. The fixed bank at $C000-$FFFF is banks 14-15 (PRG $1C000-$1FFFF).

## 1. Getting into a level without playing

- **Path** (`src/path.ts`): Start held frames 700-703 skips the title's drawing, Start 824-827 picks
  "Start" on the menu; the level card (LEVEL n / MISSION / TRIES / GET READY) shows ~848-1150, the
  level is up by 1180. Proved by frames every 10/20 (runs/s3, regenerated by `src/shots.ts`).
  Start pressed earlier (frame 404) is ignored; on the half-drawn title (704) it only finishes it.
- **The level cell is `$A8`.** (`src/level-scan.ts`, then `src/level-poke.ts`)
  - Setting a cell on the menu changes no level (Start resets them): only menu options ($07A5,
    $07A7) and junk.
  - Set on the card (frame 900), of 794 cells read between card and level and holding ≤ 16, `$A8`
    = 1 and 2 changed the level's first screen (scan stopped after `$A8` was found: `$3F`, `$371`
    gave broken/menu screens, not levels).
  - `$A8` = 0..11 on the card loads 12 different levels (runs/level-poke/*.png, one each); the cell
    keeps the value. 12 = the size of the tables the loader indexes with it (§2).

## 2. The level loader (B15:$E9D5, fixed bank)

Read in the disassembly, every value confirmed by `src/who-writes.ts` (frame 1150, level 1:
writer pc and the ROM byte each RAM cell's value was loaded from):
- `LDY $A8`; `$C031,Y` → `$8A`/`$89` (PRG bank of the level's blocks, also put in R6);
  `$C03D,Y` → `$8B` (PRG bank of the layout). `$C019 + 2·$A8` → `$9E/$9F` = the level header.
- `$04..$26` ← header bytes `$0C..$2E` (LDA ($9E),Y; level 1: `$04/$05` = $832B from PRG $1F8C).

| $A8 | header | blocks bank $8A | layout bank $8B |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 / 1 / 2 | $9F80 / $9FC0 / $9BC0 | 0 | 1 / 1 / 0 |
| 3 / 4 / 5 | same | 2 | 3 / 3 / 2 |
| 6 / 7 / 8 | same | 4 | 5 / 5 / 4 |
| 9 / 10 / 11 | same | 6 | 7 / 7 / 6 |

## 3. Who writes the play field (`src/vram-origins.ts`, `src/taint.ts`)

Nametable writes from the card to frame 2400, level 1, Right held from 1180 (taint: each value
followed back to the ROM through registers and RAM):
- **B15:$EF43** (`STA $2007` at $EF61 / $EF71) writes the field a column at a time ($2000 = $0C,
  +32): 22 rows, and 8 more when the address high byte ≥ $24, from the RAM buffer at `($3B)` =
  $0500. The values come from ROM bank 0 ($8300-$8BFF) — copied there by B15:$EB3F.
- B15:$F229..$F2D1, B6:$B36D: the status bar (digits from immediates, panel from bank 13).
- B15:$F31F/$F33A: from bank 0 $91C0-$92FF and RAM $0600 — not yet looked at.

## 4. Building a column strip (B15:$EB3F, $EB83)

- 8 blocks one under the other (`$46` bit 0 clear) or side by side (set), into $0500: 4 columns
  of 32 bytes.
- Block number: `LDA ($7E),Y` with R6 = `$8B` (layout bank); `$7E` = `$18/$19` + column, +$40
  for each block down — the layout is 64 blocks a row.
- Block's 16 tiles: `($04) + 16·block`, R6 = `$8A`, row-major 4 × 4 (scatter table $EBE0).
- Block's attribute byte: `($06),block` → `$3E`, stored at `($41)` (the attribute buffer).

## 5. Pages: the world is 240 px pages of 8 layout rows (`src/level.ts`)

- Read as plain 32-px block rows, the decoder matched a ladder descent in level 1 (Right to X 860,
  Down; `src/experiments/down-check.ts`) only 77.5 %: below world Y ≈ 240 the screen is the map moved up
  16 px. Read as pages — 30 tile rows a page, 8 layout rows a page, the 8th only its top 16 px —
  the same frames match 100 % (1 073 160 samples).
- All 12 headers agree (`src/experiments/hdr.ts`): the start camera is a multiple of 512 in X and 240 in Y,
  and the start layout pointer `$18` equals layout + 64 · (8 · page + row in page) + column for
  every one — including levels 5, 8, 9, which start at Y = 480, 480, 240.
- The builder's own test of the half row: `StripUp` keeps the row in the page in `$3D`, B15:$E924
  compares it with 7.

## 6. The check, level 1 (`src/check.ts`, `src/check-run.ts`)

On each frame (at line 241), for every tile centre of the field (lines 0-175, x 8-247): its world
place (camera of the previous frame + screen position) and its nametable cell (the scroll the PPU
drew that line with); tile number and palette compared with the decoder. The world → nametable
relation is not assumed: the grids are only checked to line up (0 misaligned samples).

Every nametable write is tagged with where it came from, following the game's buffers (taint of
our own, B15:$EB94 → $EBCB/$EBDA → $0500 → $EF61/$EF71/$EFA4; $EB57/$EB65 → $0600 → $F31F/$F33A):
- a screen cell last written by anything else (the status bar) is counted apart ("not built");
- a screen cell holding a write made for **another** place of the world is "stale": the game had
  not rewritten that cell yet. Seen when the camera crosses a page edge (the top two rows of the
  next page are rewritten some frames late) and after a teleport, when the player falls far and the
  camera outruns the builder. Not a difference of the format: the write itself was right for its
  own place;
- every builder write is checked against the decoder at its own place, seen or not.

Two wrong turns, kept as lessons: (1) a camera teleported to any multiple of 32 broke the builders'
invariants (the first screen always starts at nametable column 0, row 0) — real starts are on the
512 × 240 grid, so the teleports' scroll copy is set to 0 and Y is a page top; (2) "stale = equals
the tile a whole page away" was dropped: on repeating wall texture it matched by chance; the
provenance tags replaced it.

**Teleports** (`src/teleport.ts`): at B15:$EAE1 (the first screen about to be built, the header
already copied) the camera, `$18`, the game's scroll copy and the player are set; the screen is then
built and scrolled by the game. Proved harmless: a teleport to the real start gives the same RAM as
the real start, byte for byte (`src/experiments/tpdiff.ts`, 0 bytes differ outside the stack).

Level 1, `check-run.ts 1 --teleport --frames 6000 --per 900 --step 96` (a seeded wander: a
direction 20-120 frames, B half the time, A 5 %):

| part | frames | samples | matched | stale | match | stale apart | tile writes | attribute writes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real start, 6000 frames | 6 000 | 3 957 150 | 3 957 150 | 0 | 100 % | 100 % | 18 136 / 18 136 | 9 715 / 9 715 |
| 108 teleports × 900 frames | 94 367 | 60 635 563 | 60 252 645 | 382 918 | 99.368 % | 100 % | 257 588 / 257 588 | 77 393 / 77 393 |

- Cells: 34 379 of 61 440 seen; of the 32 096 not in void blocks, 25 595 (79.7 %). 0 cells ever
  differed; 1 745 were seen only stale or also stale.
- Background CHR on every field line = header `$26`..+3 (0 lines other); palette `$3F00-$3F0F` =
  the header's on every counted frame (0 frames other); background pattern table always $0000.
- 2 833 frames left out (fewer than half the samples agree, or nothing of the level to compare):
  cards after a death, the tally screen after the exit.

## 7. Boss rooms: levels 3, 6, 9, 12 (header $9BC0 of each group)

- Drawn with "as many rows as the layout bank holds", their lower part is noise: their layouts sit
  in the blocks bank ($9555, $93FD, $957F, $9453), among other tables.
- What the game reads (`src/experiments/boss-extent.ts`, 6000 frames from the real start): level 3
  camera X −4..322, Y 0, layout rows 0-7; level 6 X 41..256, Y 0..130, columns 0-16, rows 0-10;
  level 9 X −66..341, Y 240..354, rows 7-18; level 12 X 437..768, Y 0..529, columns 13-32, rows
  0-24. Rows are still 64 blocks apart.
- The camera has no limits of its own: it follows the player (B15:$FAE1…$FCF3; one axis at a time,
  `$2D`, switching only on the grid, `$2A` / `$2B` = 0); `$EE`, which the follow code tests, is the
  way the player faces (B14:$CFEE / $D001 from the pad bits in `$28`). The room's walls in the
  layout are its limits.
- "The layout ends where the next table of a header begins" (`src/experiments/layout-end.ts`)
  fails: level 12's would end after 22 rows, yet rows 23-24 are shown and match.
- The check from the real start, 6000 frames each: levels 3, 6, 9, 12 — 100 %, 100 %, 100 %,
  99.998 % (100 % with stale cells apart); every tile and attribute write matched. Level 12: the
  palette is not the header's on 3 945 frames, and one frame's 176 field lines had other CHR banks.

## 8. No-clip: what lies outside the rooms (`src/noclip.ts`)

- **The exit.** Player held at Y 64 and moved right in level 3: at X ≈ 482 the level ends
  (fade, "WELL DONE"). First-run code by frame (`src/experiments/exit-code.ts`) leads to B15:$E267
  (`$52` = 2 fade, then `INC $A8`), reached only from B13:$B9CB: B13:$B99F takes the player within
  32 × 32 px of (`$FE24 + 2·$A8`, `$FE3C + 2·$A8`) — the exits table, fixed bank. Exits (X, Y):
  1 (1904, 1504), 2 (1760, 1728), 3 (488, 48), 4 (1882, 1744), 5 (1920, 1504), 6 (−32, 304),
  7 (128, 1776), 8 (1920, 1504), 9 (688, 304), 10 (128, 1744), 11 (1824, 1744), 12 (1016, 512).
  Level 1's matches the tally reached by a teleport to (1792, 1440); level 3's the no-clip run;
  level 6's is out of the world (its way out is something else — not looked at).
- **A wrong conclusion corrected.** §7 said the camera "has no limits": the camera that stopped at
  X 343 in the first no-clip try had in fact stopped because the exit fired. Still true: no camera
  bounds were found in the code read (B15:$FAE1…$FCF3); the follow code reads the player's screen
  X as one byte (`$7C`, B15:$FAC0) and moves ≤ 3 px a frame — a player put off-screen sends it the
  wrong way (seen: camera X 5965).
- **Interventions** (all in `noclip.ts`): player X/Y written at line 241 every frame, led just
  ahead of the camera at 2 px a frame (`lead`); the CPU sent from B13:$B9CB to its RTS at $B9CE (no
  exit); health `$074A` = $32 and timer minutes `$0746/$0747` = 0, 4 held (B14:$D6A5 takes health
  for long falls). The game builds and scrolls the screens itself; the check counts as always.
- **Level 3** (6.3 M samples, 100 %; 64 820 / 64 820 tile writes, 33 920 / 33 920 attribute
  writes): the camera went X −19..1797, Y 0..1036. Besides the playable corridor (top left) the
  layout holds a second, walled room no one can enter (X ≈ 830-1340, Y ≈ 30-390), then strips and
  noise — other tables of the bank read as layout. The game shows all of it as the decoder draws it.

## 9. Past the right edge: the layout read on as one array (found by the user in the player)

The user flew right past the layout's last column and the screen "came round": the level's left
part again. `src/experiments/wrap-right.ts`: no-clip in level 1 to camera X 2146, 2304, 2546, 2816,
3328 (Y 0, camera at rest 20 frames): every visible tile right of world X 2048, 704 of 704 at each,
is the layout byte at (row + 1, column − 64) — the next row's start —, against 90-242 for "the same
row again". The layout pointer `$18` is only ever stepped by ±1 and ±$40 (B15:$EFDD, $F0A8, $F01B,
$F05E): there is no row width in the code beyond the $40, so a column past 63 is the next row's,
as one to the left of 0 is the previous row's (§6: $7FFF).

## 11. The camera goes up and down only in odd 256-px bands (found by the user in the player)

No-clip in level 3 would not take the camera down at X 1133. The follow code (B15:$FAEB) looks at
the vertical at all only when the player's X high byte (`$0440`) is odd; otherwise it handles X only
(`$FB1F`). `src/experiments/camera-down.ts`: player held at camera + (124, 168) with camera X 1133
(band 4): 240 frames, the camera stays (1135, 0), `$2E` never set. Camera led to X 1300 first
(band 5): within 20 frames it aligns to X 1280 (`$2A` = 0), `$2D` becomes 1 and it goes down at
3 px a frame (Y 594 at frame 220). So vertical travel happens with the camera on a multiple of 256
in an odd band. The player shows the band and says which way the camera can go.

## 12. Down forever: the layout pointer goes round the whole CPU address space (found by the user)

From level 3's room, no-clip straight down: the room comes back at camera Y 30 720.
`src/experiments/wrap-down.ts` (band 1, camera X 256): `$18/$19` is $9555 at page 0 and grows by
$200 a page (8 rows × 64) — $B55D at page 16, $F55D at 48, $155D at 64 (past $FFFF), $955D at 128
= Y 30 720, the start again (+8 columns: the camera's X). 65 536 / 512 = 128 pages × 240 px.
On the way the strip builder reads as "layout" whatever the CPU sees: $A000-$BFFF (the bank in R7
then), the fixed bank's code $C000-$FFFF, RAM $0000-$07FF and its mirrors (live), the PPU and APU
registers ($2000-$5FFF — reads of $2002/$2007 change the PPU's state), cartridge RAM $6000-$7FFF.
Only the layout bank's 8 KB is fixed data: `Level` reads the layout bank alone (`& 0x1FFF`), so
the pictures stop at the bank's end and say nothing about pages past it. The room seen again is not
playable: its walls and objects are not where the player stands (Y 30 816) — they are not the
layout (not looked at further).

## 13. The whole address space as layout (`src/address-space.ts`, viewer switch, off by default)

128 pages from the layout's first byte, every CPU address taken as a block number. ROM bytes from
the cartridge through the banks the builder had mapped; the rest (RAM, registers, cartridge RAM)
as the builder read it in one no-clip descent through all 128 pages (band 1), elsewhere the memory
of the level's first frame — those are a moment of play, not level data, and are drawn dimmed.
The check of the model, level 3: 4 256 ROM bytes read by the builder in the descent, every one the
cartridge's at the bank assumed; the banks seen were one each — 13 at $A000, 14 at $C000, 15 at
$E000. Pictures: `out/levels/<nn>-space-<k>.png` (not in git: big, made again by the script).

## 14. Prisoners: object type 6 (`src/objects.ts`, viewer layer "пленники", on by default, toggled)

- By the code: the header's object table ($1E) gives each object X, Y and a type; the loader puts
  the type in `$04E0,X` (B15:$EAB0). Every frame B14:$C5A3 jumps through the handler table at
  B13:$A5D5 by type (bank 13 at $A000 there: checked, every dispatch in 1 500 frames). Type 6's
  handler is B14:$DFBB: with the object's `$04C0` set it marks the slot (`$0400,Y` = 8), takes one
  off `$0747` and adds one to `$068A` — the prisoners left ("Pr:" on the status bar, from header
  $2F) and the prisoners rescued. Other decrements of `$0747` (B14:$CE3A, $CE5E) lie behind type 5's
  handler ($CD85); type-5 objects stand only at X 3840, out of the world.
- On every level the number of type-6 objects equals header byte $2F
  (`src/experiments/prisoners-count.ts`): 4, 5, 0, 5, 5, 0, 6, 7, 0, 4, 5, 0 — none in boss rooms.
- In play (`src/experiments/prisoner-look.ts`, level 1, no-clip): the player put on object 19's X/Y
  — `$0747` 4 → 3, slot 19's flags → 8, `$068A` 0 → 1; object 17 was rescued on the way there (its
  slot 8, `$0747` 3). On objects 1 (type 1) and 2 (type 3) nothing. Objects 20 and 21 were not
  brought on screen by the flight (camera stopped above them) — not tried.
- Object X/Y are world pixels, as the camera's and the player's (the player's own start comes from
  the same table, object 0).

## 15. The prisoners' sprites (`src/prisoner-sprites.ts`, on the maps under "пленники")

Per level, a no-clip flight from prisoner to prisoner; the first one wholly on screen and away from
the player is taken with the camera at rest — the player held mid-screen, in the camera's dead
zone (screen X $68-$98, B15:$FB2D), 60 frames, and the camera checked unmoved — from the game's
sprite buffer at $0200 (the page B15:$EC79 sends to the PPU), entries within 16 px across and
−32…+24 px down of the object, the player's own left out; drawn with the sprite palette and the
CHR banks of their lines. All 8 levels with prisoners: 7 sprites, 16 × 32 px, at (−8, −15) from
the object's X/Y. Two tries before were wrong and are kept as lessons: taken on the move, the
offset wandered with the camera's speed (−6 … −14); holding the player where he stood, the camera
went on following and never came to rest on 4 levels.

## 16. Untouchable: the game's own invulnerability timer held (`$03C8`)

Enemy contact is tested by B14:$C79F → $C78C, which lets a hit through only while `$03C8` is 0
($C794); the loader sets it to $64 at a level's start (B15:$E9CA) and a hit sets it again. Held at
$64 (`src/experiments/untouchable.ts`, level 1, the same 6000-frame wander): without — 3 hits
(B14:$C7D5 run), 6 health lost; with — 0 hits, 0 lost. Other health losses: falls (B14:$D1F1,
$D6C6) and B14:$CBA4 (a drain every 64 frames, from $CB4E — not looked at). In the player: "E".

## 17. Collision, read in the code (B14:$C1CC-$C58E; not yet checked by experiment)

Every test takes a point near the player, turns it into a layout byte (B15:$EC00: column X/32;
page = (Y/16)/15, the page's low byte from $EC58; row in page; `$8F` = Y within the block) and
looks the block up in header tables:
- `$0A`: a class a block → B15:$FE54 an 8-bit mask, one bit a 16 × 8 cell (bit = 2 · (Y/8 in the
  block) + (X/16 in the block), $C1C4); walls skip classes ≥ $0E (B14:$C24E);
- `$0E`: a list of blocks ending in $FF — ladders (B14:$C29F at Y+16, $C2CA at Y−32);
- `$10`: pairs (block, flag) — floors to stand on, the flag set = only the block's top half
  (B14:$C556 at Y+16);
- `$14`: pairs (block, shape) — slopes, 4 × 4 grids of 8 px at B14:$C427, bit 7 = mirrored
  (B14:$C497 at Y+12);
- `$06` (the attribute table) = $FF also tested as a flag (B14:$C306, $C333, $C373).
For the shortest paths the game itself is used as the physics (§18), not a model of these.

## 18. Shortest paths: the game as the physics (`src/paths.ts`) — in progress

- Moves measured (`src/experiments/moves.ts`, level 1 start): walking 2 px a frame; **B jumps**
  (a tap: a hop of 6-12 px; held 48 frames: 44 px up, with a direction 48 px across in ~46 frames,
  1 px a frame across while in the air); A fires; down, up, select do nothing on open ground.
- `$DE` is the player's state: 0 standing, 1 falling, 2 landing (`src/experiments/fall.ts`).
  Walking off the platform's end at (372, 560) the player falls onto a **lift**: standing
  ($DE = 0) while carried between Y 560 and 672 at 1 px a frame.
- Ladders are the header's `$0E` blocks; blocks with attribute $FF are the duct corridor to the
  exit (`src/experiments/climb-blocks.ts`, drawn over level 1).
- The search (walk 4-96 px, jumps of three heights each way, climbs 4-64 frames and the diagonals,
  waits for lifts; a place settled when still 8 frames or standing 16): level 1 reaches 600
  places, 1 prisoner of 4 (19), no exit. Not reached: beyond **doors** (e.g. X 447-480 at Y 560,
  a barred column) — how a door opens is not known; firing at it from a no-clip-placed player
  showed nothing (that player did not move at all there — the placing, not the door, is suspect).
  A recorded play of level 1 is asked of the user to learn the moves the search lacks.

## 20. A TAS's trail (outside source, the user's; for where a player goes, not for the format)

(MD5 of PRG+CHR, `src/tas.ts`). Played on this NES it goes through the menu right (difficulty 2)

player's X/Y, `$A8` and `$0747` every frame; `src/tas_trail.py` → out/levels/tas.json, drawn on
the maps ("путь TAS"). Frames per level: 4 572 (with the menus), 8 112, 1 267, 3 463, 3 146,
1 530, 8 199, 6 000, 979, 7 957, 4 349, 663. Prisoners: all rescued on every level but 5, where
